Model 2: Ozempic — Swing vs Literal

Ozempic is the slimmed-down version of Model 1 (Models/Model_Full_Configs). It keeps Model 1’s backbone (weighted polls, fundamentals, correlated simulations) but exposes only a handful of weights for the team to argue about, pulls everything live from VoteHub and Kalshi, and reports two numbers:

  1. P(Democrats hold at least 51 seats)
  2. Predicted number of Democratic seats (mean of 10,000 simulations, including caucusing independents)

It comes in two variants that are identical except for how the fundamentals are calculated. Both are here so the group can compare and choose.

Ozempic_Swing Ozempic_Literal
Fundamentals 2024 state margin + national swing since 2024 Weighted average of 2024 state margin, generic ballot, approval (8 : 4 : 1)
P(D ≥ 51) 58.0% 47.7%
Predicted D seats 51.3 (median 51, 80% range 48–55) 50.5 (median 50, 80% range 47–55)
Same model with Kalshi weight 0 57.1%, 51.3 seats 46.6%, 50.4 seats
Kalshi on its own 63.5% (control market), 51.7 seats (sum of race odds) same

Carried over unchanged from Model 1: the Senate map, holdover seats, incumbent status, Osborn’s 80% chance of caucusing with Democrats, the undecided-voter treatment, poll error constants, Student-t errors with a shared tail, and the region + lean correlation structure. Keeping these the same means the difference between the models comes from the changes above, not from the simulation machinery.

How each race’s number is built

polls_s   = weighted average of the state's polls in the last 28 days (after the partisan shift)
fund_s    = Swing or Literal formula (section 3)
kalshi_s  = Φ⁻¹(Kalshi P) × σ_s       <- the margin that would give Kalshi's probability under our own uncertainty

margin_s  = (10·polls_s + 5·fund_s + 1·kalshi_s) / 16       (weights renormalize if a source is missing)

So a race with polls gets 62.5% polls, 31.25% fundamentals and 6.25% Kalshi. A race with no polls in the window (Colorado, Delaware, Oregon, New Jersey, Virginia, Kentucky, Louisiana, Tennessee, Oklahoma, Idaho, West Virginia, Wyoming) gets 83% fundamentals and 17% Kalshi.

Kalshi. Kalshi has markets for all 35 races. The notebook reads each race’s Republican market and uses P(opposition) = 1 − P(R), which handles three-way races such as Nebraska (Osborn) and Montana (Bodnar). Kalshi’s tickers are not consistent: Kentucky is under SENATELA-26, Louisiana under KXSENATELA-26NOV, and Florida and Ohio under SENATEFLS-26 and SENATEOHS-26. Each race’s ticker is therefore spelled out in SETTINGS["KALSHI_EVENTS"], and all 35 were checked by hand on Oct 26. At weight 1 of 16, Kalshi moves P(51+) by about one point. Its main use is the comparison.

3. The two fundamentals formulas

Notation: margin2024_s = Harris − Trump in the state; GB = generic-ballot margin (D − R, two-party); AP = approval converted to a margin = 0.35 × −(net approval); inc = ±3 toward an incumbent on the ballot, 0 for open seats.

Today’s national inputs are the same in both variants: GB = D+9.0 and net approval −22.3 → AP = D+7.8.

Swing (Ozempic_Swing)

national_now = (2·GB + 1·AP) / 3                       = D+8.6
swing        = national_now − (Harris−Trump nationally, 2024 = R+1.5)   = +10.1 toward D
fund_s       = margin2024_s + swing + inc

This is a uniform swing: every state starts from its own 2024 result and moves by however much the country has moved since 2024. The weights (GB 2, approval 1) decide only how the two national signals average into “today’s national environment”. The 2024 state margin is the starting point, not a weighted term.

Literal (Ozempic_Literal)

fund_s = (8·margin2024_s + 4·GB + 1·AP) / 13 + inc

This is the blackboard formula taken at face value: a weighted average of the three numbers.

Worked examples

State 2024 Swing fundamentals Literal fundamentals
Maine (Collins running, −3) D+6.9 6.9 + 10.1 − 3 = D+14.0 (8·6.9 + 4·9.0 + 7.8)/13 − 3 = D+4.6
Texas (open) R+13.7 −13.7 + 10.1 = R+3.6 (8·−13.7 + 36 + 7.8)/13 = R+5.0
Wyoming (open) R+45.8 −45.8 + 10.1 = R+35.7 (8·−45.8 + 43.8)/13 = R+24.8

4. Why the variants disagree

The two formulas treat the 2024 baseline differently.

  • Swing passes the full national shift (+10.1) through to every state.
  • Literal uses GB and approval as stand-ins for the state’s own margin, with 5/13 ≈ 38% of the weight. It also never subtracts 2024’s national result, so in effect it does two things at once:
    1. It shrinks every state’s lean by 38% toward zero. Wyoming’s R+46 becomes R+25.
    2. It adds only 38% of today’s national margin, not the full change since 2024.

Setting the two equal shows where they cross:

margin2024 + 10.1  =  (8·margin2024 + 43.8) / 13      →   margin2024 = R+17.5
  • For every state bluer than R+17.5 in 2024, Literal is more Republican than Swing. That covers every competitive race this year: Maine, Michigan, Ohio, Texas, Iowa, Alaska, North Carolina, Georgia, New Hampshire, Minnesota.
  • For states redder than R+17.5, Literal is more Democratic. Those races are all Safe R either way, so the extra Democratic margin wins nothing.

That asymmetry explains the 10-point gap in P(51+). Literal costs Democrats ground exactly where control is decided and gives it back where it doesn’t matter.

A second way to see it: under Literal, a perfectly neutral national environment (GB = 0, AP = 0) would still put Wyoming at only R+28 and Massachusetts at only D+15. The formula builds in a pull toward 50-50 for every state, whatever the national mood.

5. Arguments for each

For Swing - It is the standard way fundamentals models use past results (uniform national swing). Its assumptions are easy to state: states keep their relative partisanship, and the whole map moves with the national mood. - Units are consistent. A state margin plus a change in national margin is still a state margin. Literal averages a state number with two national numbers, which are different quantities. - The weights mean what they say: GB 2 : approval 1 is “how do we measure the national mood?” - It matches the meeting priorities: 2024 state margin “heavily” (it is the full baseline), generic ballot “heavily” (2 of 3 of the national signal), sentiment “weighted less”.

For Literal - It is exactly what we wrote on the board, which makes it easy to explain in the presentation. - Shrinking toward the national average is a crude form of regression to the mean. 2024 was a single, candidate-specific election, and Senate races are less nationalized than presidential ones (Collins, Osborn, Peltola and Brown all run ahead of their party). Partly forgetting 2024 has some logic. - It leans less on a single national number. If the generic ballot is overstating Democrats, Literal is hurt less.

Caveats on both: the polls carry 62.5% of each race’s margin, so the fundamentals formula only matters where polls are thin or disagree with fundamentals. Maine, Michigan and Minnesota are where it matters most (section 6).

If the group likes Literal’s “don’t trust 2024 completely” idea but not its side effects, a middle ground is Swing with a lower W_FUNDAMENTALS. That trusts the polls more without shrinking safe states toward 50-50. The sweep shows P(51+) rising from 58% to 66% when W_FUNDAMENTALS goes from 5 to 2.5.

Literal is very sensitive to its fundamentals weights because they change both how much state lean is kept and how much of the national environment gets through. 8. Data notes and known limitations

  1. VoteHub has very few national polls. Only 2 Trump approval polls and 3 generic-ballot polls fall in the 4-week window. When the window has fewer than 5, the most recent 5 are used, so approval currently draws on polls back to late August. The old National_Polls.xlsx had 34 approval polls from Sep 21 to Oct 14. It was left out per “use just VoteHub”, but adding it back would firm up the approval signal. Approval has a small weight in both variants, so this changes the fundamentals by well under a point.
  2. Nominee table. VoteHub lists candidates by name, not party, so each poll is matched to SETTINGS["NOMINEES"] by surname. The table came from Kalshi’s market list on Oct 26. Idaho, Montana and South Dakota use “highest-polling non-Republican” (the Sheet2 independent rule), which picks Bodnar (MT) and Bengs (SD) when they poll ahead of the Democrat. Polls without both nominees are dropped (e.g. NH Pappas vs Scott Brown, MA Moulton vs Deaton). In the current window that removed none.
  3. Independents other than Osborn (Bodnar, Bengs, Achilles) are counted as Democratic seats if they win. All three are below 10% on Kalshi, so this barely matters, but it should be stated.
  4. “Percent Error” on VoteHub’s scorecard is used as the accuracy measure. VoteHub doesn’t say whether it is error on vote share or on margin, so only the relative values are used (each pollster vs the median), not as a size of poll error.
  5. Kalshi as a margin. Converting a probability to a margin uses the model’s own race SD. Kalshi’s 98–99% markets are clipped to 99%, which slightly pulls safe races toward the middle. None of those races are close, so this has no effect on control.
  6. Race SD assumes the three sources’ errors are independent. That is optimistic: polls and Kalshi are correlated. The resulting SDs (about 4–5 pts with polls, 6.4 without) are close to Model 1’s, so the two models are comparable, but the true uncertainty is probably a bit larger.
  7. Fixed weights can’t tell good data from thin data. A race with one poll gets the same 62.5% poll weight as Texas with 14. Model 1’s precision weighting handled this automatically. This is the main price of the simpler design.

9. Decisions for the group

  1. Swing or Literal (the reason both exist). Swing currently gives 58.0% / 51.3 seats; Literal gives 47.7% / 50.5.
  2. Polls 10 : Fundamentals 5. This balance moves the answer more than anything else. Fixed weights can’t account for how many polls a race has (limitation 7).
  3. Kalshi. At weight 1 it is effectively a comparison. If it is meant to “account for events and candidate quality” (Alaska, Texas, Nebraska), it would need roughly weight 3–5 to have any visible effect. The sweep shows P(51+) going from 58% to 62% (Swing) at weight 5.
  4. Approval data: VoteHub only (as now), or add National_Polls.xlsx back for approval (limitation 1).
  5. Incumbency for appointed senators (Moody, Husted, Darline Graham): currently the full 3 pts, the same as elected incumbents. Model 1 gave them half.
